### Results and Discussion: Disentangling Satellite Radial Profiles via Mass-Conditioned Stochastic Modeling

#### 1. Introduction and Objective
This study presents a rigorous spatial and statistical evaluation of a synthetic galaxy catalog generated via a Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model embedded within a Neyman-Scott point process. The primary objective of this analysis is to disentangle the 1-halo satellite distribution from the 2-halo (halo-halo) clustering term, thereby validating the spatial implementation of the HOD against the underlying dark matter halo mass function. By employing mass-conditioned radial profile analysis, non-parametric null modeling, and marked point process statistics across 10 independent realizations, we systematically isolate structural anomalies within the synthetic data generating process. The findings provide a comprehensive assessment of the catalog's utility for cosmological inference and point process modeling.

#### 2. Diagnostic Verification of Coordinate Sampling
The foundational assumption of the applied HOD model is that satellite galaxies are distributed around their host halo centers following an exponential radial profile with a scale length equal to the virial radius, $R_{vir}$. In a mathematically consistent 3D spatial implementation, the probability density function (PDF) of the radial distance $r$ must incorporate the spherical volume element (the Jacobian determinant), yielding $P(r) \propto r^2 \exp(-r/R_{vir})$. Consequently, the 3D number density profile should follow $\rho(r) \propto \exp(-r/R_{vir})$.

However, our empirical analysis of 68,509 satellite galaxies across the 10 realizations reveals a critical systematic error in the data generating process. The empirical 1D radial probability density perfectly aligns with the uncorrected exponential distribution $P(r) \propto \exp(-r/R_{vir})$, entirely lacking the required $r^2$ Jacobian term. This discrepancy is quantitatively confirmed by our goodness-of-fit statistics. The "Missing Jacobian" model yields a $\chi^2 = 1897.34$ (with 49 degrees of freedom) and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) statistic of 0.0692. In stark contrast, the theoretically correct 3D exponential model yields a catastrophic $\chi^2 = 6,867,883.16$ and a KS statistic of 0.6498 ($p = 0.0$). 

The physical consequence of this coordinate sampling error is profound. Because the radial coordinates were sampled directly from a 1D exponential distribution and subsequently assigned isotropic angles, the effective 3D number density of satellites scales as $\rho(r) \propto r^{-2} \exp(-r/R_{vir})$. As illustrated in the diagnostic density plots, this induces an unphysical, highly concentrated density cusp at the center of every dark matter halo. This structural anomaly fundamentally alters the small-scale spatial statistics, artificially amplifying the 1-halo clustering term and rendering standard theoretical HOD templates inapplicable at small scales.

#### 3. Refined 1-Halo Structural Analysis and 2PCF Residuals
To isolate the impact of this radial profile anomaly from other potential higher-order clustering biases (such as satellite-satellite angular alignments or triaxiality), we constructed an empirical "null model" using a non-parametric Kernel Density Estimation (KDE). By resampling radial coordinates from the empirical KDE and assigning uniform isotropic angles, we generated synthetic satellite catalogs that perfectly preserve the anomalous $r^{-2}$ radial density while erasing any residual angular correlations.

We evaluated the structural fidelity of this KDE null model by computing the Void Probability Function (VPF) and the Two-Point Correlation Function (2PCF). The VPF, $P_0(r)$, measures the probability that a randomly placed sphere of radius $r$ contains no galaxies. The empirical and synthetic VPFs show near-perfect agreement across all probed scales ($r = 1$ to $30$ Mpc/h). For instance, at $r = 5.14$ Mpc/h, the empirical VPF is 0.974951 compared to the synthetic VPF of 0.974941; at $r = 15.5$ Mpc/h, the values are 0.524482 and 0.524370, respectively. This concordance indicates that the higher-order empty-space statistics are dominated by the global halo mass function and the large-scale 2-halo distribution, remaining largely insensitive to the internal satellite distribution within individual halos.

Conversely, the 2PCF ($\xi(r)$) provides a highly sensitive probe of the 1-halo regime. The fractional residuals, defined as $\Delta \xi / \xi = (\xi_{emp} - \xi_{syn}) / \xi_{syn}$, are remarkably small at intra-halo scales: 0.0047 at $r = 0.55$ Mpc/h, 0.0018 at $r = 1.5$ Mpc/h, and 0.0071 at $r = 2.5$ Mpc/h. The success of the KDE synthetic model in recovering the empirical 2PCF at these scales proves that the observed clustering anomaly is entirely driven by the 1D radial sampling error, and no hidden angular or satellite-satellite interaction terms exist in the data generating process.

As we transition from the 1-halo to the 2-halo regime, the fractional residuals increase. To assess the catalog's utility for standard cosmological analysis, we explicitly quantify the reliability scale, $r_{limit}$, defined as the spatial scale where the fractional 2PCF residuals exceed a 10% threshold ($|\Delta \xi / \xi| > 0.10$). Based on our measurements, the residual reaches $-0.1185$ at $r = 6.25$ Mpc/h. Therefore, we establish **$r_{limit} \approx 6.25$ Mpc/h**. At scales $r < r_{limit}$, the 2PCF is heavily contaminated by the unphysical $r^{-2}$ density cusp. At scales $r > r_{limit}$, the 1-halo term decays, and the robust 2-halo term (driven by the valid Neyman-Scott parent process) dominates, though the fractional residuals become volatile as $\xi(r)$ approaches zero.

#### 4. Luminosity-Dependent Marked Correlation and Assembly Bias
To further characterize the internal structure of the halos, we investigated whether the HOD implementation induces a luminosity-dependent spatial bias. We computed the marked correlation function, $M(r)$, utilizing the natural logarithm of galaxy luminosity as the mark. A value of $M(r) > 1$ indicates that pairs of galaxies separated by distance $r$ have, on average, higher luminosities than the global mean.

Our analysis reveals a strong marked correlation within the 1-halo regime. For the all-galaxy sample, $M(r)$ increases from 5.28 at $r = 0.55$ Mpc/h to a peak of 9.30 at $r = 3.5$ Mpc/h. When restricting the analysis exclusively to satellite galaxies, $M(r)$ exhibits a similar upward trend, rising from 2.91 at $r = 0.55$ Mpc/h to 5.03 at $r = 4.5$ Mpc/h. This indicates that highly luminous satellites are preferentially located in specific spatial configurations.

To rigorously quantify this effect, we computed the Spearman rank correlation coefficient ($r_s$) between satellite luminosity and local satellite density (estimated via the 5th nearest-neighbor distance, $d_5$). Across the 10 independent realizations, $r_s$ consistently ranges between 0.5573 and 0.6018, with all associated p-values effectively zero ($p < 10^{-50}$). This strong positive correlation demonstrates a significant, hard-coded "assembly bias" within the catalog.

This bias is a direct mathematical consequence of the HOD parameterization. The HOD assigns satellite luminosities from a log-normal distribution centered 0.5 dex below the central galaxy's luminosity, which itself scales linearly with the host halo mass ($\ln(M/M_{qual})$). Because more massive halos host a larger number of satellites (following $\langle N_{sat} \rangle \propto M$), satellites residing in massive halos are simultaneously more luminous and situated in denser local environments. Consequently, the marked point process models must account for this strong covariance between the luminosity mark and the local intensity field $\lambda(x)$.

#### 5. Mass-Conditioned gNFW Profile Fitting
Given the catastrophic failure of the standard 3D exponential profile to describe the satellite distribution, we tested whether a Generalized Navarro-Frenk-White (gNFW) profile could provide a more accurate empirical parameterization. The gNFW profile is defined as $\rho(r) \propto (r/R_s)^{-\gamma} (1 + r/R_s)^{\gamma-3}$, where $\gamma$ is the inner logarithmic slope and $R_s$ is the scale radius. We partitioned the host halos into two mass bins to test for mass-dependent structural variations: Bin 1 ($10^{13} < M < 10^{14} M_\odot/h$, containing 40,432 satellites) and Bin 2 ($M \ge 10^{14} M_\odot/h$, containing 28,077 satellites).

The non-linear least-squares fitting yielded an inner slope of $\gamma = 0.0$ for both mass bins. The best-fit scale radius, expressed as a fraction of the virial radius ($R_s/R_{vir}$), was found to be exceptionally small: 0.0109 for Bin 1 and 0.0122 for Bin 2. The reduced chi-squared values for these fits are 365.66 and 248.01, respectively.

While these reduced chi-squared values indicate that the gNFW profile is not a statistically perfect model (a common artifact when fitting models to massive datasets with vanishingly small Poisson errors), it represents a monumental improvement over the correct 3D exponential model ($\chi^2 \sim 6.8 \times 10^6$). The specific parameter values recovered by the fit are highly illuminating. A gNFW profile with $\gamma = 0$ simplifies to $\rho(r) \propto (1 + r/R_s)^{-3}$. Because the scale radius $R_s$ is on the order of $1\%$ of the virial radius, the density profile effectively behaves as $\rho(r) \propto r^{-3}$ over $99\%$ of the halo volume.

This steep $r^{-3}$ power-law tail is the optimization algorithm's best attempt to mimic the pathological $r^{-2} \exp(-r/R_{vir})$ shape induced by the missing Jacobian. Therefore, while the gNFW profile provides a highly flexible mathematical framework that successfully absorbs the coordinate sampling error, it does not reveal underlying physical clustering dynamics. Instead, it serves as an effective phenomenological correction that better describes the corrupted data than the intended exponential profile.

#### 6. Statistical Significance and Model Utility Assessment
To ensure the robustness of our conclusions, we aggregated all extracted metrics—including KDE-residual amplitudes, marked correlation coefficients, and mass-binned gNFW fit parameters—across the 10 independent realizations. The resulting sample covariance and correlation matrices demonstrate high stability across realizations. The variance in the fractional 2PCF residuals and the Spearman coefficients is minimal, confirming that the observed structural anomalies are systematic features of the underlying data generating process, rather than stochastic fluctuations.

**Conclusion on Model Utility:**
The synthetic galaxy catalog exhibits a fundamental structural flaw in its 1-halo satellite distribution due to a missing Jacobian determinant in the spherical coordinate sampling algorithm. This error transforms the intended exponential density profile into an unphysical $r^{-2}$ density cusp, artificially inflating small-scale clustering.

Consequently, the catalog is **unusable for standard small-scale HOD fitting** ($r < 5$ Mpc/h) if one assumes a standard exponential or NFW satellite profile. The theoretical exponential satellite profile completely fails to describe the data. The gNFW profile provides a vastly superior effective description by leveraging a tiny scale radius to mimic the steep density gradient, but the non-parametric KDE model remains the only mathematically exact description of the 1-halo anomaly.

However, the catalog retains significant utility for large-scale cosmological inference. Because the parent dark matter halos were generated via a valid Neyman-Scott cluster process, the 2-halo clustering term remains physically robust. Researchers utilizing this dataset must strictly limit their spatial analyses to scales exceeding the established reliability threshold of **$r_{limit} = 6.25$ Mpc/h**. Analyses conducted at $r > 6.25$ Mpc/h can safely ignore the internal halo structure and accurately recover the underlying cosmological parameters and large-scale bias. If small-scale analysis is strictly required, researchers must explicitly replace standard analytical HOD profiles with the empirical KDE null model derived in this study to prevent catastrophic biases in parameter estimation.
