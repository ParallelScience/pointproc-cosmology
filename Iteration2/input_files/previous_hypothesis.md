**Title: Testing for Non-Separable Intensity via Luminosity-Dependent Spatial Segregation**

Current results confirm that the HOD model is a separable marked Neyman-Scott process where spatial distribution and luminosity marks are independent. However, this assumes a static, non-evolving galaxy population. I hypothesize that if we introduce a non-separable intensity model—specifically by testing for **luminosity-dependent spatial segregation**—we can detect subtle deviations from the current HOD that standard 2PCF and VPF analyses miss. 

Specifically, I propose to compute the **Marked Correlation Function $M(r)$ using a sliding luminosity threshold** (rather than a global mean) to determine if the most luminous satellites are more centrally concentrated within their host halos than the least luminous ones (a proxy for dynamical friction). By comparing the radial distribution of the top 20% vs. bottom 20% of satellites within the same mass-binned halos ($M > 10^{13} M_\odot/h$), we will test the null hypothesis that the spatial kernel $\lambda(r|M)$ is independent of the luminosity mark $L$. If $M(r)$ deviates from unity at small scales ($r < 0.5 R_{vir}$), it indicates that the current HOD implementation lacks the "mass-segregation" or "dynamical friction" effects present in more complex N-body simulations. This will provide a definitive test of the limits of the current separable intensity model and establish a baseline for identifying non-random galaxy evolution within the synthetic catalog.
