**Title: Quantifying the Impact of Satellite "Phase-Space" Anisotropy on the 1-Halo/2-Halo Transition**

Previous iterations identified that the current HOD implementation suffers from a 1D radial sampling bias, leading to artificially compact halos and spurious "attractive" signals in Gibbs models. While correcting the radial sampling to a 3D volume-weighted profile ($r^2 \exp(-r/R_{vir})$) is necessary for physical realism, we hypothesize that the *velocity-space* distribution of satellites (currently Gaussian with $\sigma_v \approx 150$ km/s) is implicitly coupled to the spatial distribution in a way that standard 2PCF and VPF analyses fail to capture. 

We propose to test the hypothesis that the 1-halo/2-halo transition scale ($r \approx 5$ Mpc/h) is sensitive to the "velocity-spatial" correlation of satellites. Specifically, we will compute the **Redshift-Space Correlation Function $\xi(r_p, \pi)$** and the **Cross-Correlation between satellite peculiar velocities and their host-centric radial distance**. By comparing the current "isotropic" velocity model against a "virialized" model (where $\sigma_v$ is scaled by the local gravitational potential $\Phi \propto M/r$), we can determine if the "leakage" observed in the VPF is a purely spatial artifact or if it is exacerbated by the lack of velocity-position coupling. This will provide a rigorous diagnostic of whether the HOD's spatial hierarchy is robust to dynamical effects, effectively disentangling the geometric "compactness" error from the underlying cosmological clustering signal.
