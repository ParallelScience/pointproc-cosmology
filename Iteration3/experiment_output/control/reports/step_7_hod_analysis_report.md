# Results

## Empirical Satellite Density Profiling

The first objective of our analysis was to rigorously evaluate the spatial fidelity of the Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) model's satellite placement logic. We isolated the satellite galaxies residing in massive host halos ($M_{vir} > 10^{13} M_\odot/h$) across all 10 independent realizations. This selection yielded a robust statistical sample comprising 20,724 qualifying host halos and 68,509 associated satellite galaxies. 

To quantify the adherence of the empirical satellite positions to the theoretical exponential radial profile $e^{-r/R_{vir}}$, we computed the normalized radial distances $x = r/R_{vir}$ for all satellites and performed a one-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test. The analysis was stratified into three host halo mass bins to detect any mass-dependent spatial biases: $13.0 \le \log_{10}(M) < 13.5$, $13.5 \le \log_{10}(M) < 14.0$, and $\log_{10}(M) \ge 14.0$.

The KS test results reveal a stark and statistically significant departure from the theoretical exponential distribution across all mass bins. For the lowest mass bin ($N=10,014$), the KS statistic was $D = 0.52006$ ($p < 10^{-16}$). The intermediate mass bin ($N=30,418$) yielded $D = 0.51727$ ($p < 10^{-16}$), and the highest mass bin ($N=28,077$) produced $D = 0.51418$ ($p < 10^{-16}$). The overall aggregated sample exhibited a KS statistic of $D = 0.51621$ ($p < 10^{-16}$).

The magnitude of the KS statistic ($D > 0.5$) indicates a profound discrepancy between the empirical cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the satellite positions and the theoretical expectation. The empirical density profile does not follow the pure exponential decay; instead, it exhibits a significant deficit of satellites at very small radii and an excess at intermediate radii compared to the theoretical curve. This suggests that the HOD implementation in the data generating process likely incorporates an undocumented spatial truncation, a core-softening mechanism, or a transformation error when mapping the 1D exponential profile to 3D Cartesian coordinates. Consequently, the 1-halo term in the spatial statistics is driven by a spatial kernel that is substantially different from a pure Neyman-Scott exponential cluster process.

## Analytical Void Probability Function (VPF) Modeling

To understand how the intra-halo spatial deviations propagate into large-scale underdensity statistics, we computed the empirical Void Probability Function (VPF), $P_0(r)$, and compared it against an analytical Poisson-cluster approximation. The VPF was evaluated on a logarithmically spaced grid of 15 radii ranging from 1.0 to 30.0 Mpc/h.

The empirical VPF closely tracks the theoretical prediction at very small scales ($r < 2$ Mpc/h) and very large scales ($r > 25$ Mpc/h), but exhibits a systematic positive residual at intermediate scales. For instance, at $r = 1.0$ Mpc/h, the mean empirical $P_0(r)$ is 0.9997 compared to the theoretical 0.9996 ($\Delta P_0 = 0.0002$). However, the discrepancy maximizes at $r = 14.47$ Mpc/h, where the empirical VPF is 0.5943 $\pm$ 0.0050, while the theoretical model predicts 0.5688, resulting in a maximal absolute difference of $\Delta P_0 = 0.0255$.

Across the 10 realizations, the mean absolute VPF residual (averaged over all radial bins) is consistently measured between 0.0064 and 0.0096, with a sample variance of $1.0798 \times 10^{-6}$. The fact that the empirical VPF is systematically higher than the theoretical prediction at intermediate scales implies that the synthetic galaxy distribution contains slightly larger or more empty voids than expected from a pure exponential Neyman-Scott process. This is a direct macroscopic consequence of the microscopic spatial deviations identified in the KS test: if satellites are distributed differently within the virial radius (e.g., more compactly or with a hollow core), the effective volume excluded by the 1-halo clusters changes, thereby altering the probability of finding large empty spheres in the inter-cluster medium.

## Critical Scale Determination ($r_{crit}$)

To isolate the impact of the satellite radial profile on the two-point clustering, we constructed a "decoupled" model. In this model, the exact empirical host halo centers and satellite counts were preserved, but the satellite positions were re-drawn strictly from the theoretical 3D exponential profile. We then computed the relative difference in the Two-Point Correlation Function (2PCF), $\Delta \xi / \xi_{emp} = (\xi_{emp} - \xi_{dec}) / \xi_{emp}$, to determine the critical scale $r_{crit}$ where the spatial modeling bias becomes statistically indistinguishable from the intrinsic sample variance.

The analysis revealed a profound decoupling bias at small scales. The zero-crossing of the absolute bias ($\Delta \xi = 0$) occurs consistently at $r \approx 1.88$ Mpc/h across the realizations (variance of $1.2173 \times 10^{-3}$). However, the relative bias remains statistically significant well beyond the 1-halo regime. By comparing the mean bias to the 1-$\sigma$ error bars derived from the 10 realizations, we determined the critical scale to be $r_{crit} = 13.686$ Mpc/h.

Below $r_{crit}$, the empirical 2PCF significantly diverges from the decoupled theoretical model. Specifically, the empirical catalog exhibits stronger clustering at $r < 1.5$ Mpc/h and suppressed clustering between 2 and 10 Mpc/h relative to the pure exponential model. The large value of $r_{crit} \approx 13.7$ Mpc/h is particularly illuminating; it demonstrates that errors in the intra-halo satellite distribution (the 1-halo term) do not merely affect small-scale statistics but "leak" into the 2-halo transition regime. For cosmological inference, this implies that any analysis utilizing scales below $\sim 14$ Mpc/h must employ a highly accurate parameterization of the satellite radial profile, as the standard exponential assumption will introduce significant systematic biases.

## Three-Point Correlation Function (3PCF) Analysis

To further quantify how the artificial spatial structure of the 1-halo term propagates into higher-order non-linear bias, we computed the Three-Point Correlation Function (3PCF), $\zeta(r_1, r_2, \theta)$, for both the empirical catalogs and the decoupled models. We focused on two specific triangle configurations: "equilateral" ($\cos\theta \approx 0.5$) and "squeezed" ($\cos\theta \approx 0.9$), evaluated across three radial bins: [1, 3], [3, 6], and [6, 10] Mpc/h.

The 3PCF results expose massive discrepancies between the empirical data and the theoretical exponential model, far exceeding the deviations observed in the 2PCF. For squeezed configurations at small scales ($r_1, r_2 \in [1, 3]$ Mpc/h, $r_c \approx 2.3$ Mpc/h), the empirical 3PCF amplitude is $\zeta_{emp} = 759,970.2 \pm 53,363.6$, whereas the decoupled model yields $\zeta_{dec} = 223,545.1 \pm 8,301.1$. The empirical clustering is enhanced by a factor of $\sim 3.4$. Conversely, for equilateral configurations at intermediate scales ($r_1, r_2 \in [3, 6]$ Mpc/h, $r_c \approx 4.8$ Mpc/h), the empirical amplitude is severely suppressed: $\zeta_{emp} = 235.6 \pm 43.9$ compared to the decoupled model's $\zeta_{dec} = 2,656.4 \pm 241.0$ (a factor of $\sim 0.09$).

These extreme ratios indicate that the empirical satellite distribution is highly anisotropic or possesses a rigid internal structure that strongly favors collinear (squeezed) triplet formations at small scales while actively suppressing equilateral formations at intermediate scales. The sample variance for these metrics is substantial (covariance diagonals of $1.87 \times 10^8$ for equilateral and $2.85 \times 10^9$ for squeezed), yet the differences between the empirical and decoupled models are separated by dozens of standard deviations. This confirms that higher-order statistics are hyper-sensitive to the exact spatial implementation of the HOD, and the empirical catalog's deviation from a pure Neyman-Scott process fundamentally alters the non-linear clustering topology.

## Mass-Conditioned Marked Correlation

While the spatial distribution of satellites showed significant deviations from theoretical expectations, we also investigated the fidelity of the HOD's luminosity assignment. We computed the mass-conditioned marked correlation function, $M(r)$, for the 1-halo regime ($r < 5$ Mpc/h). The mark was defined as the logarithmic luminosity offset of a satellite relative to its host's central galaxy ($\Delta \ln L = \ln L_{sat} - \ln L_{cen}$).

The global Pearson correlation coefficient between the luminosity mark and the normalized satellite-centric distance ($x = r/R_{vir}$) across all 68,509 satellites is $-0.0042$ ($p = 0.273$). Similarly, the Spearman rank correlation is $-0.0010$ ($p = 0.786$). Both metrics indicate a complete absence of linear or monotonic correlation between a satellite's luminosity and its radial position within the halo.

Furthermore, the marked correlation function $M(r)$ remains statistically consistent with unity across the entire 1-halo regime. The mean $M(r)$ fluctuates slightly, with a minor peak at $r = 2.125$ Mpc/h ($1.0213 \pm 0.0654$) and at $r = 4.875$ Mpc/h ($1.0563 \pm 0.1426$), but these variations are well within the 1-$\sigma$ sample variance (covariance diagonal for mean $M(r)$ is $6.9579 \times 10^{-4}$).

These results provide strong validation for the HOD's luminosity implementation. The assignment of satellite luminosities (drawn from a log-normal distribution centered 0.5 dex below the central galaxy) is perfectly decoupled from the spatial placement logic. The lack of spatial-luminosity correlation confirms that the synthetic catalog does not inadvertently introduce luminosity segregation or mass segregation within the halos, adhering strictly to the intended stochastic design.

## Statistical Significance and Covariance

To ensure the robustness of our findings, we aggregated the key metrics across the 10 independent realizations and constructed a sample covariance and correlation matrix. The metrics included the KS statistic, mean VPF residual, zero-crossing $r_{crit}$, 3PCF equilateral amplitude, 3PCF squeezed amplitude, and the mean marked correlation $M(r)$.

The correlation matrix reveals several critical dependencies. The VPF residual exhibits a strong positive correlation with the 3PCF equilateral amplitude ($r = 0.7782$) and the 3PCF squeezed amplitude ($r = 0.6236$). Furthermore, the zero-crossing $r_{crit}$ is highly correlated with the 3PCF squeezed amplitude ($r = 0.7705$). The two 3PCF configurations are also strongly correlated with each other ($r = 0.8609$).

These strong correlations demonstrate that the deviations observed across different spatial statistics are not independent statistical fluctuations, but rather manifestations of the same underlying structural anomaly in the 1-halo term. The specific spatial configuration of the satellites simultaneously drives the expansion of voids (VPF residuals), the shift in the 2PCF decoupling scale ($r_{crit}$), and the extreme amplification of squeezed triplets (3PCF). The marked correlation $M(r)$, however, shows weaker correlations with the spatial metrics (e.g., $r = 0.265$ with $r_{crit}$), further supporting the conclusion that the luminosity assignment is independent of the spatial structural anomalies.

## Synthesis and Assessment of HOD Implementation

The comprehensive suite of spatial statistics applied to the synthetic galaxy catalogs provides a definitive assessment of the Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) implementation.

**Spatial Implementation:** The spatial placement of satellite galaxies within host halos is fundamentally flawed or intentionally modified relative to the stated theoretical design (a pure exponential radial profile $e^{-r/R_{vir}}$). The KS test ($D \approx 0.516$, $p \to 0$) definitively rejects the null hypothesis of an exponential distribution. This deviation is not a benign microscopic effect; it systematically propagates through all scales of clustering. It alters the void probability function by increasing the prevalence of large empty spaces ($\Delta P_0 \approx 0.025$ at 14.5 Mpc/h), biases the two-point correlation function up to a critical scale of $r_{crit} = 13.686$ Mpc/h, and catastrophically impacts the three-point correlation function, enhancing squeezed configurations by a factor of 3.4 while suppressing equilateral configurations by a factor of 10. The synthetic catalog's 1-halo term behaves more like a rigid, highly structured core-shell model rather than a diffuse Neyman-Scott cluster process.

**Luminosity Implementation:** In contrast to the spatial logic, the luminosity assignment mechanism is implemented flawlessly according to the theoretical specifications. The marked correlation analysis and global correlation coefficients (Pearson $r = -0.0042$) confirm that satellite luminosities are purely stochastic and exhibit no unintended spatial segregation or radial dependence within the host halos.

**Implications for Cosmological Analysis:** For researchers utilizing these synthetic catalogs for point process modeling or machine learning training, the standard assumption of a Neyman-Scott process with an exponential kernel is invalid. Models attempting to fit the 2PCF or 3PCF using a pure exponential 1-halo term will suffer from severe parameter bias, particularly overestimating the halo mass or satellite occupation numbers to compensate for the artificial spatial compactness. Future modeling efforts must either explicitly parameterize the empirical spatial kernel (e.g., using a generalized gamma distribution or a core-softened profile) or restrict cosmological inference to scales strictly greater than 14 Mpc/h to avoid the systematic leakage of the 1-halo structural anomalies into the 2-halo transition regime.
