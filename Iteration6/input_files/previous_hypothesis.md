The observed "large-scale failure" (sub-Poissonian variance and inverted mass-bias) in the 2-halo regime is not a failure of the Neyman-Scott process itself, but a consequence of the rigid, uniform distribution of parent halos failing to account for the "exclusion zone" effect inherent in dark matter halo formation. I hypothesize that the 2-halo clustering can be restored by implementing a **conditional intensity model that incorporates a halo-exclusion constraint** (a hard-core distance $d_{min} \approx 2 \times R_{vir}$ between parent halos). By re-weighting the existing uniform parent catalog using a Gibbs-based thinning process—where the probability of retaining a halo is conditioned on the local density of neighboring halos—we can induce the missing large-scale clustering signal. I propose testing this by computing the 2PCF of the "thinned" halo catalog and comparing it against the theoretical $\Lambda$CDM halo bias $b(M)$. If the 2-halo term recovers, it proves that the synthetic catalog's large-scale deficiency is a lack of spatial exclusion (anti-clustering) rather than a lack of gravitational physics, allowing us to salvage the dataset for cosmological inference without regenerating the underlying simulation.
